⛔️ Top Requirements

  1. AI-native design workflow — hard requirement, not a nice-to-have.
  2. Strong senior-level product design craft.
  3. Strong product judgment / taste.
  4. Strategic product thinking, not UI execution alone.
  5. Ability to ideate and contribute to product direction.
  6. Strong communication, reasoning, and ability to challenge/push back constructively.

🎯 What We Are Hiring For

EverAI / Candy.ai — see 🗣️ Pitching the Company for the full story and scale. Hands-on, end-to-end design across chat experiences, character creation, monetisation and growth flows, and emerging surfaces (live/interactive features, AI video). Works directly with the Design Lead (Arni), PMs, and founders — high autonomy, often the only designer on a surface. Mobile-first.

We are not looking for someone who primarily receives requirements and turns them into polished screens. We want a senior designer who understands the product/user problem, generates and challenges ideas, makes and explains strong product/design decisions, and moves from ambiguity toward a strong solution — using AI actively in that workflow.

A separate, candidate-facing JD exists for external sharing — ask your TA contact for it.

⛔️ Screening Criteria

  • AI-native workflow — one of only four roles company-wide with an explicit mandatory AI-usage gate (alongside Senior Ruby on Rails Engineer, Senior Product Manager, and AI Cinematic Video Editor/Creator). Uses AI tools daily for design, prototyping, or content generation (Claude, Cursor, image/video gen, code-based prototyping) — real prompting, experimentation, and AI-assisted creation, not just familiarity. Can talk specifics, not aspirations.
  • Product design craft — end-to-end consumer product design, mobile-first, shipped at real scale (approximately 5+ years). Can explain the problem, constraints, options considered, decisions made, trade-offs, and final outcome — not just show polished screens. Advanced Figma and strong prototyping (design-tool and/or code-based).
  • Product judgment / taste — distinguishes a strong product experience from merely polished UI: clear reasoning, prioritization, thoughtful trade-offs, ability to simplify. Not reducible to subjective visual preference.
  • Strategic / product thinking — contributes to what should be built and why, not only how it should look.
  • Ideation — generates ideas and alternative solutions rather than waiting for fully formed requirements.
  • Communication / pushback — defends decisions, challenges weak assumptions, disagrees constructively, explains reasoning clearly, and changes their mind when evidence is stronger. Async communication quality (concise writing, brief-following) is a strong predictor for this fully remote team.
  • Ownership — can distinguish what they personally did from what the team did.
🎨

Portfolio evidence: a visually polished portfolio alone is not sufficient. Strong case studies show problem/context, the candidate's personal role, reasoning, alternatives/trade-offs, product thinking, decisions, outcome, and what changed because of their work.

Also confirms comfort with NSFW/adult product experiences (asked directly, early) and the B2B/contractor-to-full-time engagement model (confirmed explicitly on the first call, logged in writing) — see the shared Candidate Hard Gates & Red Flags guide for the general framework.

🚩 Red Flags

🚩
  • Primarily UI/visual execution with weak product thinking.
  • Portfolio shows final screens but little reasoning; vague ownership — unclear what they did vs. their team.
  • Waits for PMs to define the solution before designing; weak ideation.
  • Cannot explain trade-offs, or cannot challenge/push back constructively.
  • No meaningful current AI usage, or talks about AI conceptually without demonstrating how it changes their actual workflow.
  • Highly polished presentation masking weak ownership or product judgment.
  • Portfolio red flags: text-heavy/visual-light, messy typography, overly academic UX process (sticky-note walls, double-diamond diagrams, endless wireframe journeys before any design), not mobile-optimised, outdated device mockups, wrong work mix (branding/logos, marketing pages, Web3, dashboards, templated SaaS, corporate B2B), over-animated theatrics, dated hosting (WordPress/Squarespace/Canva), spelling mistakes or broken links.

Do not reject candidates merely for coming from an agency, consultancy, startup, smaller company, or an unconventional background — assess craft and evidence on their own merits. Be cautious with freelancers or studio owners with little in-house experience (probe commitment and ability to operate inside a team) but don't rule them out.

💬 Recruiter Screening Questions

ℹ️

Use these questions during your screening call. Include a summary of the candidate's answers in your submission notes in Ashby.

  1. What's your current situation, and why are you exploring? (Listen for counter-offer dynamics.)
  2. Why EverAI? Did you review Candy.ai and the Handbook?
    • What draws you to AI companionship as a space?
  3. Which product areas did you fully own, concept → launch, in your last role?
  4. Walk me through the single screen or flow you're proudest of — why does it work? (Craft probe.)
  5. Tell me about a time you proposed or significantly changed a product direction, rather than just executing a spec you were given. (Ideation probe.)
  6. Tell me about a time you disagreed with a PM, founder, or stakeholder on a design decision — how did you make your case, and what happened? (Pushback probe.)
  7. How do AI tools sit in your day-to-day design workflow — which ones, for what, concretely?
    • Give a recent example. How do you evaluate or correct the output?
  8. Have you designed for sensitive/NSFW categories? What was your hands-on involvement?
  9. You'd be engaged B2B/contractor initially, with potential transition to full-time — does that work for you? (Record answer verbatim; also confirm country of residence — tax/contract setup varies, don't assume from nationality.)
  10. Location / compensation expectations / notice period?
  11. What questions do you have for me?
