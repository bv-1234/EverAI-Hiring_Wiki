🎨 How to Assess Portfolios (Even If You're Not a Designer)

Candy is an extremely visual, mobile-first product — the portfolio review is the highest-signal step you control.

✅ Green Flags

  • Custom domain, built on Framer or Webflow — signals modern web fluency and care.
  • Animation & interaction shown in case studies — demonstrates how the design works, not just how it looks.
  • Strong visual and written communication — convey personality and conciseness; say less, say it well.
  • Clean, modern, premium aesthetic — sleek, often dark, bold visuals first; sans-serif type; intentional color. Visuals should grab attention before any text does.
  • At least one strong, relevant case study plus a second meaningfully related one (B2C, AI-driven, social/entertainment) — a pattern, not a one-off.
  • Demonstrated impact — outcomes, results, or clear rationale behind decisions.

🚩 Red Flags

  • Text-heavy, visual-light — walls of description before any work appears.
  • Messy typography — inconsistent sizes, busy layouts; sloppy hierarchy signals sloppy thinking.
  • Overly academic UX process — sticky-note walls, double-diamond diagrams, survey documentation, endless wireframe journeys before any design. We do light competitive research, then design; process-heavy designers struggle at our velocity.
  • Not mobile-optimised — broken or cropped on a phone. Mobile is our primary platform.
  • Outdated device mockups (old iMacs, iPhone 7/X frames, cluttered desk scenes).
  • Wrong work mix — dominated by branding/logos, marketing pages, Web3, dashboards, templated SaaS, or corporate B2B. These rely on established patterns; Candy routinely invents interaction models that don't exist anywhere else.
  • Vague ownership — unclear what they did vs. their team.
  • Over-animated portfolio theatrics — spectacle compensating for weak core work.
  • WordPress / Squarespace / Canva hosting — signals dated taste.
  • Spelling mistakes or broken links — instant confidence killer.

📋 Screening Protocol — Non-Negotiable Process Rules

Each rule exists because skipping it has cost us real time.

  1. Confirm B2B/contractor in the FIRST screen and record the exact Q&A in the ATS. This has burned late-stage processes 5+ times. Also verify the candidate's country of residence (tax/contract setup varies — don't assume from nationality).
  2. Log exact current compensation and expectations against €80–120k in the first screen. Note whether comp is their primary motivator.
  3. Set the full process in writing up front: recruiter screen → hiring-manager interview (Arni) → scoped case study (~4–5 hrs, clear written brief, includes a ≤5-minute Loom walkthrough with working audio) → founder calls (Thomas, Mike) → references. Everyone goes through the full process — no fast-tracks, no skipped steps, regardless of seniority, referral, or counter-offer pressure. A candidate repeatedly probing to shorten or skip steps is a culture-fit flag: log it, don't accommodate it.
  4. Ask about notice periods and counter-offer risk early. External deadlines do not compress our process; if a candidate's timeline can't survive our normal pace, we'd rather know in the screen.
  5. Referrers stay fully outside the process — no involvement in task selection, feedback, or candidate communication. Route all candidate questions to Arni/your TA contact only.
  6. Assess async communication quality throughout — concise writing, brief-following, respect for stated formats. It predicts how they'll operate on a fully remote team.

💬 Screening Questions

ℹ️

Use these questions during your screening call. Include a summary of the candidate's answers in your submission notes in Ashby.

  1. What's your current situation, and why are you exploring? (Listen for counter-offer dynamics.)
  2. Which product areas did you fully own, concept → launch, in your last role?
  3. Walk me through the single screen or flow you're proudest of — why does it work? (Craft probe.)
  4. How do AI tools sit in your day-to-day design workflow — which ones, for what, concretely? (Must-have probe.)
  5. Have you designed for sensitive/NSFW categories? What was your hands-on involvement?
  6. You'd be engaged B2B/contractor initially — does that work for you? (Record answer verbatim.)
  7. How do you keep design consistency across multiple surfaces while moving fast?
  8. What draws you to AI companionship as a space?
