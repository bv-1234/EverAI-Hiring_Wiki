Quick Decision Guide

Scan this first. Full detail and probes are below.

⛔ Stop / Do Not Progress

Clear hard-gate failures.

  • NSFW / explicit-content refusal where the role requires exposure
  • Contract / working-model incompatibility
  • Location / timezone incompatibility with the role's requirement
  • Compensation materially outside approved range, no flexibility
  • Conflicting full-time employment where the role requires full-time commitment
  • English insufficient to perform the role's actual responsibilities

🔍 Explore / Verify

Needs evidence or clarification before you decide.

  • Cannot separate personal ownership from team results
  • Claims scale or seniority without evidence
  • Cannot show measurable impact
  • Hands-on capability is stale or delegated
  • Adjacent experience presented as direct experience
  • Answers too unclear to assess competence
  • Excessive negativity/blame, or repeated short tenures without a coherent explanation

✅ Positive Signal

Strengthens a candidate — not automatically required.

  • Strong career progression / increasing scope
  • Specific, credible examples with measurable outcomes
  • Clear personal-vs-team distinction
  • Practical AI usage with good judgment
  • Operating independently; building tools/workflows
  • Admits mistakes and explains what changed after

⛔ Admin / Eligibility

Objective requirements. Check these early.

RequirementHard stop whenConfirm
NSFW / explicit content Not comfortable working for an adult / explicit-content product, or unwilling to handle explicit content where the role requires direct exposure. Comfort working in an explicit-content environment (script below).
B2B / working model Refuses the required contractor setup, or cannot practically work under it. B2B acceptance, country of residence.
Location / timezone Cannot meet the role's location requirement or working overlap, or needs relocation/visa support the role doesn't provide. Against the role brief's exact requirement.
Compensation Expectations materially outside the approved range, and not flexible. Expectations against the published range.
Availability / commitment Timing genuinely doesn't work, or candidate intends to retain another full-time job where full-time is required. Notice period, earliest start date, competing commitments.
English Insufficient to perform the actual responsibilities of the role. Ability to work and communicate in the role's required language, in context.
💬

NSFW confirmation script: "Our products can include explicit / NSFW content. Are you fully comfortable working in this environment as part of your day-to-day role?"

🌍

Location default: Europe, or a nearby timezone with strong overlap with European working hours, unless the individual role brief explicitly says otherwise. The role-specific rule always wins over this default.

Notes

  • Previous adult-industry experience is not required unless the role brief says otherwise.
  • Use the role brief for the exact location/timezone requirement.
  • Asking about compensation is not a red flag — ask for expectations and range alignment, never current or previous salary.
  • A long notice period or side project is not automatically a rejection.

🚩 Hard Skills / Evidence

These are evidence problems. The role brief defines the exact technical or functional bar.

1. Ownership is unclear

Relies on "we", "the team", or company-level outcomes; can't isolate their own contribution.

Probe: What was specifically yours? Which decisions did you personally make? What did someone else own?

2. Scale is presented without personal control

Large company, team, or project numbers are presented as personal achievements without evidence of direct control or contribution.

Probe: What part was directly under your control? What did you personally change? What measurable outcome followed?

3. No concrete, measurable impact

Describes responsibilities but can't explain outcomes, even when repeatedly asked — e.g. revenue, growth, conversion, retention, quality, reliability, delivery speed, product usage, risk reduction, operational efficiency, cost reduction, or other role-relevant KPIs.

A senior candidate who repeatedly avoids measurable outcomes is a stronger version of this same gap.

4. Claims don't reconcile, or numbers don't add up

Facts shift across CV, application, and interview, or figures don't make sense when examined (timelines, team size, budget, scope).

Probe: Walk me through this timeline again. How does that number break down?

5. "NDA" used as a blanket excuse

Confidentiality is real, but it shouldn't prevent describing their own role, decisions, and approach in general terms.

Probe: Without naming the client/product, what did you personally decide, build, or change?

6. Hands-on capability is no longer current

Now mainly manages, coordinates, advises, or delegates work that the role requires them to execute personally.

Probe: What did you personally execute recently? Which relevant tools/workflows/processes do you still use yourself? When did you last do this work hands-on?

Seniority itself is not a red flag.

7. Adjacent experience is presented as direct experience

Examples: management presented as execution, strategy presented as delivery, reporting presented as analysis ownership, advisory work presented as operational ownership, coordination presented as functional expertise.

The role brief defines what counts as relevant experience.

8. Overlapping or stacked roles

Multiple concurrent roles/employers on the timeline without a clear explanation of how each was handled.

Probe: How did you manage both at once? What did each one actually involve?

🎯

Company name, title, or pedigree are sourcing signals — not proof of fit. Always validate ownership, depth, recency, scale, results, and decision-making. Use evidence and context — don't automatically reject on any single item above.

⚠️ Cultural Fit / Soft Skills

Signals to explore. Do not reject on these alone without evidence.

Poor preparation / low engagement

Did they review the company/role? Can they explain why they're interested? Do they show useful curiosity?

Do not judge based on visible energy or extroversion.

Low ownership in ambiguity

Can they move forward without a complete playbook? Can they make reasonable decisions independently? Can they ask for clarity without becoming blocked?

Cannot discuss mistakes constructively

Can they identify something that failed? Can they explain what they learned or would change? Do they get defensive when challenged?

Persistent blame / disrespect
Immediate concern when clearly demonstrated

Discriminatory/degrading comments, disrespect toward recruiters/interviewers, hostility or repeated dismissiveness.

Explore when

Every previous employer/manager is blamed; candidate takes no ownership in conflicts; cannot discuss difficult experiences constructively.

Repeated short tenures

A question, not a rejection criterion. Check: layoffs, contracts, acquisitions, restructuring, project-based work, role mismatch, other reasonable explanations.

Ask: what led to each move?

Communication prevents assessment

Can you understand what they did, what they owned, why they made decisions, and what happened? Do they repeatedly avoid answering the actual question asked?

Perfect English, charisma, or presentation style are not the standard unless the role explicitly requires them.

🤖

AI usage is generally a positive signal, not a company-wide hard gate. Only treat it as mandatory when the individual role brief explicitly says so.

✅ Positive Signals

Useful evidence that strengthens a candidate — not automatically required.

Strong career progression / increasing scope Specific and credible examples Clear personal-vs-team distinction Recent achievements with measurable outcomes Strong ownership Explains decisions and trade-offs Learns quickly Good product/company preparation Strong questions Practical AI usage with good judgment Builds tools/workflows to solve problems Evidence of operating independently Admits mistakes and explains what changed after

None of these are hard requirements — they're what makes an eligible, evidence-backed candidate stand out.

✅ Not a Red Flag by Itself

Asking about compensation or benefits Asking about workload / working hours Asking detailed process questions Being laid off Career break Contract / freelance experience Consulting background Short tenures with reasonable explanation No previous adult/NSFW industry experience unless the role requires it No AI background unless the role requires it Introverted / lower-energy communication style Respectfully challenging assumptions Asking for clarity Coming from outside target-company lists Non-traditional career path Different job title with equivalent responsibilities No promotion / lack of career progression

Assess evidence against the role brief, not proxies.

Before You Submit

  • Eligibility: Are all admin hard gates cleared?
  • Role fit: Do they meet the role-specific hard-skill bar?
  • Ownership: What did they personally own?
  • Evidence: What concrete evidence supports the submission?
  • Concerns: Is any meaningful concern still unresolved?
⚠️

If you cannot answer these five questions, do not submit the candidate yet.

Individual role briefs override this page for technical requirements, level, compensation, location, mandatory domain experience, and role-specific hard gates.
