How to use this guide

This guide applies across EverAI roles unless a role-specific brief explicitly says otherwise.

We separate concerns into three categories:

  • Admin / Eligibility — objective requirements that can make a candidate ineligible.
  • Hard Skills / Evidence — whether the candidate can demonstrate the level of ownership and capability required for the role.
  • Cultural Fit / Soft Skills — behavioral signals to explore before making a decision.

Important: not every red flag is an automatic rejection. Where something is marked as a Hard Stop, do not progress unless the role brief explicitly allows an exception. For softer signals, ask before concluding. If a concern is confirmed, document the evidence in Ashby.

Role-specific briefs always take precedence for technical requirements, seniority thresholds, target companies, compensation, location, and process details.

1️⃣ Admin / Eligibility

These should be checked early. They are objective requirements rather than subjective culture judgments.

NSFW / Explicit-Content Discomfort

EverAI operates products that can include explicit sexual content and adult themes. Candidates must understand the reality of the product and be comfortable working with it at the level required by their role.

🛑

Hard Stop when

  • Candidate states they are uncomfortable working for an adult-content product.
  • Candidate is unwilling to work with explicit content where the role requires direct exposure.
  • Candidate becomes hesitant or changes their answer after the nature of the product is explained.
ℹ️

Always ask clearly. Use this wording:

"Our product operates in the AI companionship space and can include explicit / NSFW content. Are you fully comfortable working in this environment as part of your day-to-day role?"

For roles involving direct exposure to explicit content, make that clear before asking for confirmation. Do not infer comfort from previous industries — confirm it directly.

Contract / Working-Model Mismatch

EverAI roles are generally offered through a B2B contractor arrangement unless the specific role says otherwise.

🛑

Hard Stop when

  • Candidate will only accept permanent employment and the role is B2B-only.
  • Candidate cannot legally or practically work under the required setup.
  • Candidate expects an employment structure the role cannot support.

Confirm: B2B acceptance, country of residence, and relevant contracting constraints where applicable.

Do not assume eligibility from nationality alone.

Location / Timezone Mismatch

Each role has its own geographic and timezone requirements.

🛑

Hard Stop when

  • Candidate cannot meet the stated location requirement.
  • Candidate cannot provide the required working-hours overlap.
  • Candidate expects relocation or visa sponsorship when the role does not provide it.

Use the role brief for the exact requirement.

Compensation Mismatch

Compensation should be discussed against the published range for the role.

🛑

Hard Stop when

  • Candidate's expectations are materially outside the approved range and they are not flexible.
  • Candidate expects a package structure that the role does not offer.

Do: ask for compensation expectations.

Do not: ask for current or previous compensation where this is not required or appropriate.

A candidate asking about compensation is not a cultural red flag.

Notice Period / Availability Mismatch

Confirm notice period, earliest start date, and any known constraints.

A longer notice period is not automatically a rejection. It becomes a problem only where the role has a genuine start-date requirement.

Conflicting Full-Time Commitments

Where the role requires full-time commitment:

🛑

Hard Stop when

  • Candidate intends to retain another full-time job.
  • Candidate cannot commit the required working capacity.
  • Candidate's external work materially conflicts with the expected role.

Side projects or advisory work should not automatically be treated as a problem. Assess the actual conflict.

2️⃣ Hard Skills / Evidence

The exact technical bar varies by role. This section covers the common evidence standards we expect across EverAI.

Cannot Separate Personal Ownership from Team Results

Candidates should be able to explain what they personally did, what decisions they personally made, what they changed, what the wider team owned, and what the measurable outcome was.

Red flag language: "We increased revenue...", "The team managed...", "We launched...", "Our budget was..." — without being able to identify their own contribution.

Probe: What part of that was specifically yours? Which decisions did you personally make? What was owned by someone else?

Claims Scale Without Evidence

Large numbers alone are not evidence of seniority or ownership. Company revenue, total team budget, agency portfolio value, customer volume, total users, and department results must not be presented as if the candidate personally controlled them.

Probe: What part of that number was directly under your control? What did you personally change? Which metric changed because of your work?

No Measurable Impact

Strong candidates should be able to connect their work to an outcome relevant to the role — depending on the role, this may include revenue, CAC/CPA, ROAS, LTV, retention, conversion, purchase rate, product usage, speed, reliability, scale, quality, risk reduction, or operational efficiency.

Red flag: candidate repeatedly describes responsibilities but cannot explain results.

Current Role Is Too Far from Execution

Many EverAI roles require people who still do the work, not only manage it.

Explore when: senior title, large team, heavy management scope, agency oversight, strategic-only role, or execution delegated elsewhere.

Probe: What did you personally execute in the last week or month? Which tools/platforms/code/workflows do you still use yourself?

Do not reject someone because they are senior. Reject only when the required hands-on capability is no longer current.

Relevant Experience Is Stale

Previous experience can still be valuable, but some roles require current familiarity with fast-changing tools, platforms, or markets.

Explore: How recently did they do the relevant work? What are they doing now? Are they still current with the relevant tools/processes?

The role brief should define where recency is a hard requirement.

Strong Company or Title, Weak Actual Depth

A target company is only a sourcing signal. A prestigious company, senior title, or recognizable brand does not override role-specific requirements.

Always qualify: actual responsibilities, personal ownership, hands-on depth, scale, and results.

Cannot Explain Decisions or Trade-Offs

We expect candidates to explain why they made a decision, not only what happened.

Probe: What alternatives did you consider? Why did you choose that approach? What information changed your decision? What would you do differently now?

Adjacent Experience Presented as Direct Experience

Examples vary by role: management presented as execution, strategy presented as delivery, account management presented as acquisition, reporting presented as analysis ownership, advisory work presented as operating ownership.

Role-specific briefs define what counts.

3️⃣ Cultural Fit / Soft Skills

These are generally signals to explore, not automatic rejection criteria. Use observable behavior and evidence rather than subjective impressions.

Poor Preparation / Low Engagement

Possible signals: did not review the company or role despite having the opportunity, cannot explain why the role interests them, asks no meaningful questions, demonstrates very little curiosity about the product or work.

Ask: What attracted you to this opportunity? What did you review before the call? What stood out to you about the product?

Avoid judging based on personality style or visible "energy." The concern is preparation and genuine interest, not whether someone is extroverted.

Low Curiosity About AI / Product

EverAI is an AI company. Candidates do not all need the same technical AI depth, but they should show reasonable curiosity about the product and category.

Explore: What interests you about AI in this space? Have you experimented with any AI tools relevant to your work?

For roles where AI-tool usage is a hard skill, follow the role-specific brief rather than this general guidance.

Difficulty Taking Ownership in Ambiguity

EverAI operates in a fast-moving environment. Explore when a candidate appears to require highly detailed instructions for every task, extensive approval before acting, fixed processes before making progress, or unusually narrow role boundaries.

Ask: Tell me about a time you had to solve something without a clear playbook. What did you decide yourself?

Do not equate preference for structure with lack of ability. Look for actual evidence.

Cannot Discuss Mistakes Constructively

We value candidates who can examine decisions that did not work.

Ask: Tell me about a decision that didn't work. What did you learn? What would you do differently now?

The issue is not whether they have failed. The concern is an inability to recognize or learn from mistakes.

Persistent Negativity About Previous Employers

Leaving a poor environment is not a red flag. Complaints about legitimate issues are not automatically a concern.

Explore when a candidate: blames every previous team or manager, takes no ownership of any conflict, speaks disrespectfully about colleagues, or cannot articulate anything they learned from difficult experiences.

Ask: What worked well in that environment? What would you have handled differently?

Repeated Short Tenures

Multiple short roles are a question, not a rejection criterion. There may be legitimate reasons: layoffs, company closures, contract roles, acquisitions, restructures, project-based engagements, or poor role fit.

Always ask: What led to each move?

Document the explanation rather than making assumptions.

Disrespectful or Inappropriate Behavior

This is one of the few behavioral areas that can become an immediate concern when clearly demonstrated: disrespect toward recruiters, coordinators, candidates, or interviewers; discriminatory or degrading comments; hostility when challenged; repeated dismissiveness; or inability to receive reasonable feedback professionally.

Assess actual behavior, not communication style or personality preference.

Communication Prevents Assessment

Perfect communication is not required. The concern is when the candidate cannot communicate clearly enough for us to understand what they did, what they owned, why they made decisions, and what the result was.

For roles requiring strong external or executive communication, the role brief may set a higher standard.

Expectation Mismatch

Explore whether the candidate's expectations align with scope, seniority, level of ownership, pace, working model, compensation, and career direction.

Do not treat questions about salary, benefits, workload, working hours, or process as negative signals by themselves. The concern is a material mismatch after the role has been explained.

4️⃣ What Is NOT a Red Flag by Itself

The following should not be treated as automatic negative signals:

  • Asking about salary or benefits
  • Asking about working hours or workload
  • Asking detailed questions about the hiring process
  • Having been laid off
  • A career break
  • Contract/freelance experience
  • Multiple jobs where there is a reasonable explanation
  • Not having worked in adult/NSFW before
  • Being introverted or less visibly enthusiastic
  • Challenging an assumption respectfully
  • Wanting clarity before making a decision
  • Coming from a company outside our target-company lists
  • Having a non-traditional career path
  • Not matching a preferred title exactly

Always assess against the actual role requirements.

5️⃣ Agency / Recruiter Screening Rule

Before submitting a candidate, you should be able to answer:

  • Are they eligible for the role?
  • Do they meet the role-specific hard-skill bar?
  • What did they personally own?
  • What measurable evidence supports the submission?
  • Is there any confirmed red flag or unresolved concern?
⚠️

If you cannot answer those questions, do not submit the candidate yet. Complete the relevant screening before sending the profile.

6️⃣ Final Reminder

ℹ️

This page is the shared EverAI baseline.

The role-specific brief defines the exact technical bar, target-company guidance, seniority requirements, screening questions, and rejection criteria for that role.

Never progress or reject a candidate based only on a single generic red flag without understanding the context, unless the requirement is explicitly marked as a Hard Stop.
