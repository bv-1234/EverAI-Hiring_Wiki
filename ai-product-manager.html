🧑‍🧑‍🧒‍🧒 Team & Environment Context

  • The Role: A highly hands-on, technical Product Manager who sits at the intersection of product, AI, data, and engineering. Not a typical PM, but rather a builder/prototyper: rapid iteration on A/B tests, growth experiments, feature shipping, and coding and pushing changes to production. Reports to Pierre Gerbaud (Product Lead).
  • The Focus: Heavy emphasis on rapid experimentation and AI-native building — using AI coding tools to prototype, test, and ship mid-size features and growth experiments fast, rather than writing a spec and handing it to engineering.
  • The Bar: Maker mindset. High agency, independent, motivated — moves fast with minimal oversight and unblocks rather than waits.

🎯 Calibration Anchors & Target Profiles

See 🚩 Candidate Hard Gates & Red Flags for company-wide screening rules (NSFW gate, location/timezone default, English proficiency, compensation-expectations-not-history, evidence standards). Below is what's specific to this role.

⛔️ Non-Negotiables

  1. NSFW comfort — 100% dealbreaker; must be genuinely comfortable building products based on uncensored models and content.
  2. AI-native builder — experience using AI coding tools (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, or similar) to build, code, analyze data, prototype, contribute through pull requests, and ship functional product changes; has personally shipped a real, functional prototype, experiment, or feature end-to-end. This means a PM who personally builds and experiments with AI tools — not simply a PM who has worked at an AI company or writes specs for AI features and hands them to engineering.
    📎

    Professional evidence is strongly preferred; an exceptional side project may still count if it shows production-level complexity, iteration, and ownership — it should not be just a landing page or toy demo.

  3. Data fluency — connects metrics to product decisions, defines success measures, analyzes results, and proposes or ships iterations.
  4. Demonstrated curiosity about AI tools — independent AI-tool exploration is non-negotiable (limited use is a red flag 🚩).
  5. Bias for action / agency — independent, hungry, fast-moving; capable of owning ambiguous work through delivery and measurable impact.
  6. Tight cross-functional collaboration — works directly with designers and developers, particularly on web products, while remaining technically autonomous.
🔒

AI usage is a hard gate for this role. Company-wide, AI usage is generally a positive signal rather than mandatory (see Candidate Hard Gates & Red Flags) — but Senior Product Manager is one of only four roles where it's an explicit requirement, alongside Product Designer, Senior Ruby on Rails Engineer, and AI Cinematic Video Editor/Creator. Do not submit a candidate who cannot show hands-on, personal AI-building experience for this role.

✨ Nice-to-have

  • Design fluency — can prompt a decent UI to stay autonomous (valuable, but not a blocker for an otherwise strong candidate)
  • Ideally, background at a fast-scaling D2C/consumer tech company or scale-up, with experience on consumer-facing features — B2C, entertainment, or gaming industries especially

The first four pools cover background fit only — they don't guarantee AI-coding fluency. The PM-as-builder pool (strongest for AI-native fluency) skews B2B/dev-tools, not B2C. The two preferences may not always show up in the same candidate.

This list is a sourcing aid, not an automatic qualifier or rejection rule — a strong candidate from outside it should still be considered.

🚫 Do not source from direct AI-companionship/AI-dating competitors (Replika, Character.AI, and similar AI companion products/apps) — based on experience, this is not what we're looking for.

📎 Always ask for a portfolio, GitHub profile, repository, live project, demo, or a concrete project example shipped with AI — this is the single best signal for the AI-Native Building & Shipping category, and should be requested as a standard part of screening.

📈 Growth trajectory > experience — don't over-index on seniority or years of experience. A candidate with real agency, curiosity, and momentum — even if not deeply experienced — can be a stronger fit than someone more senior without that trajectory. Motivated and curious matters more than a polished résumé. A non-traditional path into PM is welcome, not a concern: a developer or a designer, for example, who grew into product may fit this profile well.

❓ Why does a non-traditional path fit this well?

Classic PM career progression is built around not coding — writing specs, running standups, delegating to engineers. The people who've spent years doing that are the population least likely to have picked up hands-on AI-coding fluency, precisely because that wasn't their job. The people who do have that fluency right now tend to be recent converts — people who just started operating in a PM-adjacent capacity, not people with a multi-year PM career behind them.

💬 Screening Questions

ℹ️

Use these questions during your screening call. Include a summary of the candidate's answers in your submission notes in Ashby.

  1. Our product involves AI companionship and uncensored, adult-oriented content. Are you fully comfortable building and shipping features in this category?
  2. Have you tried Candy.ai? What do you think about it?
  3. Why are you considering a move now?
  4. Walk me through a specific feature or prototype you personally built and shipped using AI coding tools — how did you use AI to write code, analyze data, or contribute pull requests, and what was your process?
  5. What was the highest impact (on revenue or similar) feature you've worked on? What was your role in it?
  6. Can you talk us through an example of how you first defined metrics to measure, then measured and reported results of a new feature against those metrics?
  7. What AI tools or techniques have you sought out and tried on your own initiative, beyond what a specific project required?
  8. What are the top 2–3 things you're looking for in your next opportunity?
